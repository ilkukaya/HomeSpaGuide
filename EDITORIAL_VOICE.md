# HomeSpaGuide Editorial Voice & Compliance Rules

This file governs every word of customer-facing content on the site.
Violating these rules creates legal liability under FTC 16 CFR Part 255
(Endorsement Guides) and Amazon Associates Operating Agreement. Read it
before writing or editing anything published to readers.

## What we are

A curated editorial affiliate publication. We research, compare, summarize,
and recommend products in the home spa category. We send readers to Amazon
to buy.

## What we are NOT

- A testing laboratory
- A consumer research organization with measurement equipment
- A team that owns and trials every product we cover
- An accredited certification body
- A first-person "I bought this and here's how it went" review blog

## Banned claims

Never assert any of the following in any content unless we can produce
written documentation backing it:

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| "We tested" / "Our tests show" | We don't test. FTC violation if false. |
| "We measured X dB / X°F / X kWh / X gallons" | Specific measurements imply lab work. |
| "We ran it for X days / weeks" | Implies long-term hands-on trial. |
| "In our long-term testing" | Implies multi-month ownership. |
| "We held a [meter/calibrated instrument]" | Implies equipment we don't have. |
| "Our expert technicians" | Implies staff we don't have. |
| "Lab-tested" / "Independently tested by us" | Implies certification. |
| "Compared in our lab" | We have no lab. |
| "Side-by-side trial" | If we didn't actually own both, no. |
| "Our durability test of 6 months" | We don't do durability tests. |

## Approved framings

Use these instead. They're honest AND they convert just as well — they
position us as a curator the reader trusts, which is what we actually are.

| Use this | When |
|---|---|
| "We selected this because…" | Justifying an editor pick |
| "Manufacturer's published spec…" | Citing official numbers |
| "Owner reviews on Amazon consistently report…" | Citing aggregated feedback |
| "[Brand] claims [X]. Owner reports broadly support this." | Brand claim + social proof |
| "Based on the spec sheets, [A] beats [B] on…" | Spec comparison |
| "Across multiple Reddit threads in r/hottubs…" | Citing community feedback |
| "The category generally suffers from…" | Category-level truth |
| "Most buyers in this price range will find…" | Buyer-profile framing |
| "If your priority is [X], this is the cleanest fit because…" | Recommendation logic |

## How to write a product review

A product page is NOT a first-person review. It is:

  1. A 1-sentence positioning statement (who it's for)
  2. The manufacturer's headline specs, summarized
  3. The 3–5 things owners praise most consistently
  4. The 2–3 things owners complain about most consistently
  5. Our curator's take on who should buy it vs. who shouldn't
  6. A spec comparison vs. 2 alternatives in the same category
  7. A clean Amazon CTA

No "we tested". No invented measurements. No first-person ownership
narratives unless an editor actually owns the product and discloses that.

## The "Editor Score" is a curation index, not a measurement

A 0–10 number that synthesizes:
  - Quality signals (build, materials, manufacturer track record)
  - Value (price-to-feature ratio in category)
  - Setup (owner-reported ease)
  - Durability (long-term owner feedback themes)
  - Energy efficiency (published energy claims + owner bill reports)

Each sub-score is editorial judgment, not a test result. The score can
and should differ from Amazon's average star rating — but the
methodology is honest curation, not lab work.

## Pricing rules

Never quote a specific live price. Always use the price range bucket
defined in the product schema (under-100, 100-250, 250-500, 500-1000,
1000-2500, 2500-5000, 5000+). The "Check price on Amazon" button
delivers the live price.

## Disclosure rules

Every blog post, every guide, every product page must carry the affiliate
disclosure. The footer disclosure is necessary but not sufficient — it
must also appear above the fold on review/guide content.

## When in doubt

Ask: "Could I show this claim to an FTC investigator and produce
documentation backing it?"

If yes → publish.
If no → rewrite as a curation/owner-feedback claim, OR delete it.
