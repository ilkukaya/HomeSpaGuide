Intex is the brand most likely to come up first when someone Googles "inflatable hot tub". They were not the first to ship one, but they were the first to ship one that didn't feel like a kid's pool with a heater bolted on.

The current **PureSpa** range — bubble (AirJet), jet (HydroJet) and the hybrid Plus — is what most other brands are quietly benchmarking against. Build quality has stayed consistent across the last three generations: the Fiber-Tech tensile beams stand up straight, the laminated PVC takes a season or two of pool chemistry without ghosting, and the control panel is usually the only place a unit ages visibly.

Where Intex earns its keep: **support and spare parts.** Replacement pumps, covers, filter cartridges and inflatable rings are all stocked through their own US distribution, and most of them are interchangeable across model years. That matters more than any spec sheet — cheaper inflatable brands routinely disappear from Amazon inside 18 months, leaving owners with nowhere to source a replacement heating element, a complaint pattern that shows up consistently on r/hottubs.

Where Intex doesn't lead: insulation. Even the better PureSpa units pull more electricity to hold 104°F in a Minnesota February than a hard-shell tub would. If you live somewhere genuinely cold and plan to soak year-round, owner reports on r/hottubs consistently describe meaningfully higher winter electric bills versus comparable hard-shell tubs — factor that into the running cost.
