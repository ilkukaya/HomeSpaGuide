sections:
  - heading: The five-minute weekly routine
    body: |
      Most owners over-think this. A clean hot tub needs five things, done on the same day every week — pick Sunday morning if you have no opinion. Test pH and total alkalinity with a strip. Add the sanitizer top-up (one bromine tab or two chlorine tabs in a floating dispenser for a typical 200-gallon tub). Shock the water with non-chlorine MPS oxidizer to break down body oils and residue. Rinse the filter cartridge under a garden hose. Wipe the waterline with a soft cloth — this is where biofilm starts, not in the body of the water.
  - heading: Target numbers (memorize these)
    body: |
      <strong>pH:</strong> 7.2–7.8. Below 7.2 and you'll corrode heating elements; above 7.8 and the sanitizer goes lazy. <strong>Total alkalinity:</strong> 80–120 ppm. This is the buffer that keeps pH from drifting. <strong>Sanitizer:</strong> about 3–5 ppm for bromine or free chlorine (typical label range; CDC guidance for public hot tubs sets minimums of 4 ppm bromine and 3 ppm free chlorine). <strong>Calcium hardness:</strong> 150–250 ppm. Most municipal water is fine; well water often needs a pre-filter cartridge on the fill hose.
  - heading: Bromine vs chlorine — pick one and stay with it
    body: |
      Bromine is more stable at 95°F+ and easier on sensitive skin, but slightly more expensive per gallon-week. Chlorine is cheaper and the recovery is faster after a heavy soak, but it off-gasses harder at hot-tub temperatures and the smell sticks to towels. Based on this trade-off, we suggest bromine to first-time owners and chlorine to anyone with a covered tub that gets light weekly use.
  - heading: When to drain and refill
    body: |
      Every 3–4 months for typical residential use; every 6–8 weeks if more than four people use it weekly. Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) creeps up slowly and is the one thing you cannot dose your way out of. The visual signal is foam that won't break down after dosing — that's your cue, regardless of the calendar.
faqs:
  - question: Can I skip the weekly shock?
    answer: Not really. Sanitizer kills bacteria; shock breaks down the organic load (sweat, body oils, hair products) that builds up between weekly tests. Skipping the shock is the most common reason water turns cloudy mid-month.
  - question: Why does my water smell strongly of chlorine when it should be sanitized?
    answer: That smell is actually chloramines — used-up chlorine bound to organic load. It's a sign the water is under-sanitized, not over. Shock it and test again the next day; the smell should drop dramatically.
  - question: Are saltwater hot tubs lower-maintenance?
    answer: Slightly. The chlorine generator handles weekly sanitizer dosing automatically, but you still test, balance, and shock on the same schedule. Owner reports suggest the time savings are modest rather than the dramatic reduction some marketing implies.
  - question: How often should I test hot tub water?
    answer: At least once a week as part of the routine, and before each soak if the tub is used daily or by several people. Busy tubs can use up sanitizer within a day or two.
