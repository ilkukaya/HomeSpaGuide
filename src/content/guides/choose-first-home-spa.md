sections:
  - heading: Step 1 — Pick a category
    body: |
      Inflatable spas are the right starting point for most first-time buyers. They cost a fraction of what hard-shell tubs do, set up in an afternoon, and use a normal 110V outlet. The trade-off is shorter lifespan (3–6 seasons) and weaker insulation.
  - heading: Step 2 — Size for who will use it
    body: |
      The most common buying mistake is going too big. A 4-person tub fits two adults very comfortably; a 6-person tub is genuinely for entertaining and costs noticeably more to heat. Buy the smallest tub you will actually use.
  - heading: Step 3 — Plan your electrical and surface
    body: |
      110V/120V inflatable tubs need a dedicated, GFCI-protected outlet on a 15A circuit (check the manual; some specify 20A). Hard-shell tubs typically need 240V wiring and a licensed electrician. Whatever you buy, the surface beneath needs to support 3,000–4,500 lbs filled — a deck rated for that load, a poured pad, or compacted gravel.
faqs:
  - question: What's the cheapest way to own a hot tub?
    answer: A used inflatable from a reputable owner is the cheapest path. New, a compact tub like the Coleman SaluSpa Tahiti EnergySense is the rational entry point. Anything cheaper is a coin flip on durability.
  - question: How much will it cost to run per month?
    answer: Plan on $25–60/month in electricity for a 110V inflatable, depending on climate and how aggressively you set the temperature. Hard-shell tubs are often cheaper per month because they're better insulated.
  - question: Can I leave a hot tub running year-round?
    answer: Yes, if it's freeze-rated (several EnergySense SaluSpa inflatables, such as the Bestway Hawaii and Milan, include Freeze Shield anti-freeze heating) or it's a hard-shell tub. Standard inflatables should be drained and stored once nighttime temps drop below ~40°F.
  - question: Should I buy an inflatable or hard-shell hot tub first?
    answer: If you're not sure how often you'll use it, an inflatable is the lower-risk way to find out. If you already know you want year-round soaking in a cold climate, stronger jets and a tub that lasts a decade or more, a hard-shell is the better long-term buy.
  - question: What size hot tub should I buy?
    answer: Size for the people who will use it most weeks, not the occasional party. A 4-person tub comfortably fits two adults; a 6-person tub suits regular entertaining but holds more water to heat and treat.
