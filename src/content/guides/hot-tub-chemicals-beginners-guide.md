The chemicals aisle makes hot tub care look complicated. It isn't. You need a short list of products, a few target numbers and a consistent order of operations. This guide covers all three, and it applies to both inflatable and hard-shell tubs.

## What chemicals do you need for a hot tub?

**The essentials are test strips, a sanitizer, a shock, and products to raise and lower pH and alkalinity.** Everything else is situational.

### The core starter kit

| Product | What it does | When you use it |
|---|---|---|
| Test strips (or liquid test kit) | Measures sanitizer, pH, alkalinity and often hardness | Several times a week |
| Sanitizer: bromine tablets or chlorine (dichlor) granules | Kills bacteria and other germs | Continuously (bromine floater) or after each use (chlorine) |
| Shock (non-chlorine MPS or chlorine) | Oxidizes sweat, oils and organic waste | Weekly and after heavy use |
| pH decreaser (sodium bisulfate) | Lowers pH and alkalinity | When pH is above 7.8 |
| pH increaser (sodium carbonate) | Raises pH | When pH is below 7.2 |
| Alkalinity increaser (sodium bicarbonate) | Raises total alkalinity | When alkalinity is below 80 ppm |

### Useful additions

| Product | When it's worth having |
|---|---|
| Calcium hardness increaser | Soft water areas; foam or low hardness readings |
| Metal / scale sequestrant | Well water, hard water, or staining |
| Filter cleaner | Deep-cleaning cartridges every month or two |
| Line flush | Before every drain and refill |
| Clarifier | Fine haze after chemistry is balanced |
| Enzyme treatment | Tubs with frequent guests, lotions or oily waterlines |
| Defoamer | Quick cosmetic fix for foam before guests arrive |
| Bromide booster (sodium bromide) | Starting a bromine system on a fresh fill |

You can find all of these in [chemicals and water care](/category/chemicals-water-care).

## Should you choose bromine or chlorine?

**Bromine is the lower-effort choice for most beginners; chlorine is cheaper and faster-acting.** Pick one and stay with it.

Bromine holds up well in hot water and releases steadily from a floating dispenser. Chlorine (use spa dichlor granules, not pool tablets) works quickly and is usually added after each soak. Switching from bromine to chlorine generally requires a full drain. Our detailed [bromine vs chlorine comparison](/blog/bromine-vs-chlorine-hot-tub) covers the trade-offs.

## What are the target chemical levels for a hot tub?

**Keep pH at 7.2–7.8, total alkalinity at 80–120 ppm and sanitizer at roughly 3–5 ppm.** The pH and alkalinity ranges come from CDC and industry guidance; sanitizer ranges are typical label targets.

| Parameter | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| pH | 7.2–7.8 | Many owners aim for 7.4–7.6 |
| Total alkalinity | 80–120 ppm | Buffer that keeps pH stable |
| Free chlorine | 3–5 ppm | CDC sets a 3 ppm minimum for public hot tubs |
| Bromine | 3–5 ppm | CDC sets a 4 ppm minimum for public hot tubs; many owners aim higher in the range |
| Calcium hardness | ~150–250 ppm | Common manufacturer guidance; check your manual |
| Cyanuric acid (chlorine tubs) | Keep low | Builds up with dichlor use; reset by draining |

## What order do you add chemicals to a new hot tub?

**Alkalinity first, then pH, then calcium hardness, then sanitizer, then shock.** Balance the water before you sanitize it, because sanitizers work best in balanced water.

### Fresh-fill sequence

1. **Fill through the filter compartment**, ideally with a pre-filter on the hose to catch sediment and some metals.
2. **Add metal sequestrant** if you're on well water or know your water has iron or copper.
3. **Turn on the power and circulation.** Let the water mix for a few minutes.
4. **Test the water** to see your starting point.
5. **Adjust total alkalinity** to 80–120 ppm.
6. **Adjust pH** to 7.2–7.8. Alkalinity changes often move pH, so retest first.
7. **Adjust calcium hardness** if it's low.
8. **Add sanitizer.** For bromine, add a sodium bromide booster first to build the bromide reserve, then shock to activate it, then place tablets in the floater. For chlorine, add dichlor to reach the target range.
9. **Shock** the water per the label.
10. **Retest** after circulation, then heat the tub.

### Rules for every addition

- **One product at a time.** Wait 20 to 30 minutes with jets running and retest before the next.
- **Cover off while dosing.** It lets gases escape and prevents damage to the cover's underside.
- **Add chemicals to water, never water to chemicals.** Pre-dissolve in a bucket of tub water only if the label says to.
- **Pour near the jets**, not onto the shell or into the filter compartment unless directed.
- **Start with less than the label maximum.** It's easy to add more; overshooting takes extra products to fix.

## What does a weekly chemical routine look like?

**Test, balance, sanitize, shock and rinse the filter, once a week at minimum.** Test more often if the tub sees daily use.

1. Test pH, alkalinity and sanitizer.
2. Correct alkalinity, then pH, if either is out of range.
3. Top up sanitizer (refill the bromine floater or add chlorine).
4. Shock.
5. Rinse the filter cartridge.

This is the same routine laid out in [how to keep your hot tub water clean](/guides/keep-hot-tub-water-clean). If pH keeps drifting, see [how to lower pH in a hot tub](/blog/how-to-lower-ph-in-hot-tub). For filter care, see [how to clean a hot tub filter](/guides/how-to-clean-hot-tub-filter).

## How should you store hot tub chemicals safely?

**Keep chemicals in their original containers, tightly closed, in a cool, dry, ventilated place away from children and pets, and store oxidizers apart from acids.**

- **Never mix chemicals** together, even different brands of the same product type.
- **Separate oxidizers and acids.** Keep shock and sanitizers apart from pH decreaser.
- **Use clean, dry scoops**, and never share a scoop between products.
- **Keep lids tight.** Moisture can cause some products to clump or react.
- **Don't store chemicals inside the tub's equipment bay** or under the cover, where heat and humidity build.
- **Read the label** for handling guidance such as gloves and eye protection.

## What mistakes do beginners make with hot tub chemicals?

**Skipping tests, overdosing, adding several products at once and using pool chemicals are the usual beginner mistakes.**

- **Dosing without testing.** Guessing leads to overshooting, then correcting, then overshooting again.
- **Ignoring alkalinity.** Chasing pH without fixing alkalinity is the most common cause of frustration.
- **Skipping weekly shock.** This is behind many cases of [cloudy water](/blog/cloudy-hot-tub-water-fix) and [foam](/blog/hot-tub-foam-causes-and-fixes).
- **Using pool trichlor tablets.** They're too acidic and dissolve too fast in hot water.
- **Never changing the water.** Chemicals stop working as dissolved solids build up. See [how often to change hot tub water](/blog/how-often-to-change-hot-tub-water).
- **Using expired test strips.** Replace them once opened for a while or past their date.
