The filter is the hardest-working part of your hot tub's water care and the easiest one to neglect. It traps hair, skin cells, oils and debris so your sanitizer doesn't have to deal with them. When it clogs, water clouds, heaters struggle and chemicals get used up faster. The good news: cleaning it takes a few minutes.

## How often should you clean a hot tub filter?

**Rinse the filter about once a week and deep-clean it with a filter cleaner every one to three months.** Increase both if the tub is used daily.

That schedule reflects what most hard-shell manufacturer manuals recommend. Inflatable manuals often call for rinsing every few days because their cartridges are smaller.

| Task | Hard-shell | Inflatable |
|---|---|---|
| Hose rinse | About weekly | Every few days to weekly |
| Chemical soak | Every 1–3 months | Often not worth it; replace instead |
| Replacement | About every 12–24 months | Frequently; commonly every 1–2 weeks of use per manufacturer manuals |
| At every drain and refill | Deep clean or replace | Replace |

## How do you rinse a hot tub filter?

**Turn off the power, remove the cartridge and rinse it with a garden hose, directing the spray into the pleats from top to bottom.** Work around the whole filter until the water runs clear.

1. **Turn off power** at the control panel or breaker. Many manuals require this so the pump doesn't run dry.
2. **Remove the filter** from the skimmer or filter housing. Some twist out; others lift out after removing a cap.
3. **Knock off loose debris** by tapping the end cap against the ground.
4. **Rinse at a 45-degree angle** with a nozzle on a moderate setting, top to bottom, pleat by pleat.
5. **Spread the pleats** gently with your fingers to rinse deep debris.
6. **Rinse the inside core** from the center outward.
7. **Reinstall** the cartridge (or a clean spare) and restore power.

A filter-cleaning wand attachment for a garden hose makes this faster and cleaner. You'll find those in [filters and filtration](/category/filters-filtration).

## How do you deep-clean a hot tub filter?

**Soak the cartridge overnight in a bucket of water mixed with a spa filter cleaner, then rinse it thoroughly and let it dry.** This removes oils, lotions and mineral scale that a rinse can't reach.

1. **Rinse first** to remove loose debris.
2. **Fill a clean bucket** or filter-soaking container with enough water to cover the cartridge.
3. **Add spa filter cleaner** at the label dose. Liquid and granular versions both work.
4. **Submerge the filter** and soak for the time the label specifies, often overnight.
5. **Rinse thoroughly** until no suds or residue remain. Leftover cleaner can cause foam in the tub.
6. **Let it dry fully** before reinstalling, if you have a spare to use meanwhile. Drying helps loosen remaining debris and slows biofilm growth.

If your water is hard and the filter has crusty white scale, look for a filter cleaner with a descaler. Some owners follow the degreasing soak with a separate descaling soak; never mix different cleaning products in one bucket.

## Why should you keep a spare filter?

**A spare lets you rotate filters so one is always clean and fully dry, and the tub is never left running without one.** Owners on r/hottubs consistently describe longer filter life from rotation.

Filter fibers recover some shape when they dry completely, and a dry filter is less hospitable to biofilm. Rotation also means you can do a proper overnight soak without taking the tub offline. For inflatables, keeping a multi-pack of cartridges on hand is simply practical.

## What should you avoid when cleaning a hot tub filter?

**Avoid pressure washers, household detergents, bleach, dishwashers and wire brushes.** Each can damage the filter media or leave residues in the water.

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Pressure washer | Tears and flattens the filter fabric |
| Dish soap or laundry detergent | Leaves residue that causes foam |
| Chlorine bleach | Can degrade filter fibers |
| Dishwasher | Detergent residue and heat damage |
| Wire or stiff brushes | Frays the pleats |
| Muriatic acid on an oily filter | Can set oils into the fabric; degrease first if descaling |

## When should you replace a hot tub filter?

**Replace it when the fabric tears, pleats flatten or fray, end caps crack, or it stays dirty after a deep clean.** On a hard-shell, that's typically every 12 to 24 months with regular care.

Signs a filter is done:

- Pleats are soft, frayed or collapsed
- Fabric has holes or tears
- End caps or the core are cracked
- It looks discolored even after a soak
- Water clouds quickly after cleaning
- Flow warnings appear on the control panel shortly after cleaning

Always match the replacement to your tub's model number or the dimensions and fitting type printed on the old cartridge. Replacement cartridges for most major brands are listed in [filters and filtration](/category/filters-filtration).

## Are inflatable hot tub filters different?

**Yes. Most inflatable filters are small paper cartridges designed for frequent replacement rather than long-term cleaning.** Rinse them often and replace them as the manual specifies.

Small cartridges clog quickly because the water volume and bather load are proportionally high. Many inflatable owners find that swapping filters more often is the simplest fix for persistent haze. Some aftermarket reusable filters are sold for popular inflatable models; if you consider one, check that it fits and that the manufacturer's warranty terms still apply. See the [inflatable hot tubs category](/category/inflatable-hot-tubs) for model context.

## How does filter care affect water quality?

**A clean filter keeps water clear, helps sanitizer last longer and protects the pump and heater.** A dirty one undermines everything else in your routine.

If you're seeing cloudy water, a dirty filter is often the first thing to check; see [cloudy hot tub water fixes](/blog/cloudy-hot-tub-water-fix). Foam is often worsened by oils trapped in the filter; see [hot tub foam causes and fixes](/blog/hot-tub-foam-causes-and-fixes). And a restricted filter can slow heating, as covered in [how long a hot tub takes to heat up](/blog/how-long-does-a-hot-tub-take-to-heat-up).

Filter care fits neatly into the weekly routine in [how to keep your hot tub water clean](/guides/keep-hot-tub-water-clean). Pair it with the right chemicals from the [beginner's chemicals guide](/guides/hot-tub-chemicals-beginners-guide), and the tub largely takes care of itself.
