description: |
  Plug-and-play hot tubs are hard-shell spas that run on a standard 110–120V household outlet, so there is no 240V circuit or electrician to budget for. Most use a rotationally molded polyethylene shell with foam insulation and real water jets, which makes them the natural step up from an inflatable: better heat retention, a rigid seat to lean against and a longer expected lifespan. The trade-off is power — on 110V the heater and pump share a limited supply, so heat-up and recovery are slower than on a 240V spa.

faqs:
  - question: Do plug-and-play hot tubs need a special outlet?
    answer: They run on standard 110–120V power, but manufacturers typically specify a dedicated GFCI-protected circuit (15A or 20A depending on the model). Check the spec sheet before buying, and never run a hot tub on an extension cord.
  - question: Are plug-and-play hot tubs better than inflatables?
    answer: For year-round use, usually yes. A rigid shell, foam insulation and a locking cover hold heat better, and water jets give a stronger massage than air bubbles. Inflatables cost far less and pack away for winter, so they remain the better choice for seasonal or trial use.
  - question: How long does a 110V hot tub take to heat?
    answer: From a cold fill, typically a day or more to reach 104°F. Once at temperature, insulated plug-and-play tubs hold heat well, but recovery after long jet sessions in cold weather is slower than on 240V tubs.
