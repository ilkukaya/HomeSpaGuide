Built-in spa displays are not always accurate, and a floating thermometer like the Poolmaster 18307 is the cheapest way to double-check yours.

## What the manufacturer says

Poolmaster lists the 18307 as a basic floating thermometer with a shatter-resistant body, an alcohol-based indicator, a Fahrenheit/Celsius scale reading to a maximum of 120°F (50°C), a size of about 3/4" by 7-1/2", and an included polyethylene cord.

## What owners praise

- Handy check that the heater is hitting its set point
- Floats upright and is easy to spot
- Tether cord keeps it from drifting into the filter intake
- Nothing to charge or replace

## What owners complain about

- Small print is hard to read without lifting it out of the water
- Sun exposure fades the scale over time, a common trait of floating thermometers

## Who should buy it

If your priority is a cheap, reliable second opinion on water temperature, this is the cleanest fit. If you want to read the temperature from across the patio, a jumbo-numeral or wireless digital thermometer is worth the extra cost.
