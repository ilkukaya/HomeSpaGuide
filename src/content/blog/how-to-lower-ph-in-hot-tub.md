pH is the chemistry number that affects everything else. When it drifts high, sanitizer loses power, water clouds and scale starts forming on the heater. When it drops low, water turns corrosive and irritating. The fix is simple, but the order matters.

## What should the pH be in a hot tub?

**The target pH for a hot tub is 7.2 to 7.8.** That's the range cited in CDC guidance for hot tubs and in most spa manufacturer manuals, with many owners aiming for about 7.4 to 7.6.

Here's how pH fits with the other balance numbers:

| Parameter | Target range | Too low causes | Too high causes |
|---|---|---|---|
| pH | 7.2–7.8 | Corrosion, etched surfaces, eye and skin irritation | Weak sanitizer, cloudy water, scale |
| Total alkalinity | 80–120 ppm | pH swings ("pH bounce") | pH drifts up, hard to lower |
| Calcium hardness | ~150–250 ppm (common manufacturer guidance) | Foam, corrosion | Scale and cloudiness |

## How do you lower pH in a hot tub?

**Add a spa pH decreaser, usually sodium bisulfate, in small doses with the jets running and the cover open, then retest after 20 to 30 minutes.** Repeat until pH is in range.

Step by step:

1. **Test the water** with a fresh strip or liquid kit. Note both pH and total alkalinity.
2. **Check the label dose** for your tub's volume and how far you need to move pH. Most products give a dose per 100 or 500 gallons.
3. **Start with about half to three-quarters of the calculated dose.** It's far easier to add more than to correct an overshoot.
4. **Turn on the jets** and remove the cover.
5. **Pre-dissolve if the label says to**, then pour slowly into the water near the jets, away from the shell surface.
6. **Circulate for 20 to 30 minutes.**
7. **Retest** and repeat with smaller doses as needed.

Never mix pH decreaser with other chemicals in a container, and add chemicals to water, never water to chemicals. Wait until one adjustment has circulated before adding the next product.

## Should you lower total alkalinity before pH?

**Yes. If total alkalinity is above 120 ppm, bring it down first, because high alkalinity pushes pH back up after every correction.**

Total alkalinity is the water's buffer. It resists changes in pH. High alkalinity means pH keeps drifting upward and fights your decreaser. Low alkalinity means pH swings wildly with every dose.

The same sodium bisulfate product lowers both. To bias the effect toward alkalinity:

- Add the dose in a single spot with the jets **off**, so the acid concentrates briefly in one area.
- Wait 30 minutes or so, then turn the jets on to mix.
- Retest alkalinity and pH.

Lowering alkalinity also lowers pH. Aerating the water afterward (running jets with the cover open) nudges pH back up without raising alkalinity, which is a useful way to fine-tune once alkalinity is where you want it.

## How do you raise pH or alkalinity when they're too low?

**Use sodium bicarbonate (alkalinity increaser) to raise total alkalinity, and sodium carbonate, or soda ash (pH increaser), to raise pH.** Fix alkalinity first, then pH.

| Problem | Product | Also affects |
|---|---|---|
| Alkalinity low, pH low | Alkalinity increaser (sodium bicarbonate) | Raises pH modestly |
| Alkalinity OK, pH low | pH increaser (sodium carbonate / soda ash) | Raises alkalinity a little |
| Alkalinity high, pH high | pH decreaser (sodium bisulfate) | Lowers both |
| Alkalinity high, pH OK | pH decreaser, then aerate | Aeration raises pH back without raising alkalinity |
| Alkalinity OK, pH high | pH decreaser in small doses | Lowers alkalinity slightly |

Dose the same way: small amounts, jets on, cover off, retest after circulation. Soda ash can briefly cloud water if added too quickly; that usually clears as it mixes.

## Why does hot tub pH keep rising?

**High total alkalinity, aeration from jets and air blowers, and high-pH fill water are the usual reasons.** Some sanitizer systems also push pH upward.

- **High alkalinity.** The most common reason. Bring it to 80–120 ppm.
- **Aeration.** Jets and bubbles release carbon dioxide from the water, and losing CO₂ raises pH. Tubs with powerful air blowers tend to drift upward.
- **Fill water.** Some municipal and well water starts with high pH or alkalinity. Test right after filling.
- **Saltwater systems.** Chlorine generation tends to raise pH over time; system manuals usually address this.
- **Leaving the cover open for long periods.** Extended aeration and warm water both let more CO₂ escape.

## Why does hot tub pH keep dropping?

**Low total alkalinity, heavy use of acidic products and very soft fill water are the usual causes.** Low pH is less common than high pH in hot tubs but more damaging.

Heavy use of non-chlorine shock (potassium monopersulfate) can lower pH and alkalinity over time. So can bromine tablets, which are mildly acidic. If pH keeps falling, raise alkalinity to the middle of its range and see whether pH holds.

## What mistakes do owners make when adjusting pH?

**Overdosing, chasing pH before alkalinity and adjusting too many things at once are the big three.**

- **Overdosing.** Add less than you think you need. Chemistry changes take time to show up.
- **Ignoring alkalinity.** It's the root cause of most "pH won't stay put" complaints.
- **Adding several chemicals at once.** You won't know which one moved the numbers, and some react with each other.
- **Using expired test strips.** Old strips give misleading readings. Replace them once past their date.
- **Testing too soon.** Wait for full circulation before retesting.

## How often should you test pH?

**Test pH at least once or twice a week, and before each soak if the tub is used heavily.** More frequent testing catches drift before it causes problems.
