No amount of chemistry keeps hot tub water fresh forever. Sanitizers, balancers, sweat and evaporation all leave something behind, and eventually the water stops responding to treatment. Knowing when to change it saves you from fighting a losing battle with foam and cloudiness.

## How often should you change hot tub water?

**Most home hot tubs need a drain and refill every three to four months with regular use.** Heavier use, more bathers or a smaller tub means changing it more often.

That three-to-four-month figure is the interval most manufacturer manuals and water-care brands give for typical residential use. It's a starting point. A tub used daily by a family of four needs fresh water sooner than one used twice a week by a couple.

## What formula tells you when to change hot tub water?

**A common industry formula is: (tub gallons ÷ 3) ÷ average daily bathers = days between water changes.** It scales the interval to how much water you have and how heavily it's used.

"Average daily bathers" means the average number of people who soak per day across the week. Two people soaking every other day works out to one bather per day. Four people soaking every day is four.

Worked examples:

| Tub volume | Average daily bathers | Calculation | Days between changes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 250 gallons (inflatable) | 2 | 250 ÷ 3 ÷ 2 | ~42 days (about 6 weeks) |
| 250 gallons (inflatable) | 1 | 250 ÷ 3 ÷ 1 | ~83 days (almost 3 months) |
| 350 gallons (hard-shell) | 2 | 350 ÷ 3 ÷ 2 | ~58 days (about 2 months) |
| 400 gallons (hard-shell) | 1 | 400 ÷ 3 ÷ 1 | ~133 days (about 4.5 months) |
| 450 gallons (hard-shell) | 3 | 450 ÷ 3 ÷ 3 | 50 days (about 7 weeks) |

The formula is a rule of thumb, not a precise measurement. It doesn't account for guests who wear lotion, whether people shower first, or how diligently the filter is cleaned. Use it to set a reminder, then adjust based on how the water behaves.

## How do total dissolved solids (TDS) affect water changes?

**TDS is the total of everything dissolved in the water, and once it climbs too high, chemicals stop working as expected.** A common industry guideline is to drain when TDS rises about 1,500 ppm above your fill water's starting level.

To use this approach:

1. Measure TDS of the fresh water right after filling, with an inexpensive digital TDS meter.
2. Write that number down.
3. Check TDS every few weeks.
4. When the reading is roughly 1,500 ppm above the starting number, plan a water change.

Tracking the rise rather than an absolute number matters because source water varies a lot. Some municipal water starts low; well water can start several hundred ppm higher. If you use a salt system, TDS readings include the salt, so follow your system manufacturer's guidance instead.

## What are the signs it's time to change hot tub water?

**Foam that won't break down, water that stays cloudy after treatment and chemicals that no longer hold levels are the clearest signs.** Don't wait for the calendar if you see them.

- **Persistent foam.** If shock and a clean filter don't stop it, dissolved solids are likely high. See [hot tub foam causes and fixes](/blog/hot-tub-foam-causes-and-fixes).
- **Cloudiness that returns.** Water that clears after a full treatment and clouds again within days. See [cloudy hot tub water fixes](/blog/cloudy-hot-tub-water-fix).
- **Sanitizer disappears fast.** You're dosing more and more to keep levels in range.
- **pH and alkalinity won't stay put.** Adjustments don't hold.
- **Odor or skin irritation**, even with levels in range.
- **Scale forming** on the shell, jets or heater.
- **It simply looks and feels flat.** Owners across r/hottubs often describe old water as "tired," and it's a surprisingly reliable signal.

## How do you drain and refill a hot tub?

**Flush the lines, cut the power, drain, clean the shell and filter, refill through the filter well, then balance and sanitize.** Plan for a few hours plus reheat time.

1. **Run a line flush.** Add a plumbing-flush product per the label, usually the day before, and run the jets. It loosens biofilm inside the pipes.
2. **Turn off power at the breaker.** Heaters can be damaged if they run without water.
3. **Drain.** Use the drain valve or a submersible pump. Direct the water to an appropriate area, away from storm drains, and check local rules on draining treated water.
4. **Clean the shell.** Wipe with a spa-safe surface cleaner, paying attention to the waterline. Avoid household cleaners that can cause foam later.
5. **Clean or replace the filter.** This is the ideal time for a deep soak. See [how to clean a hot tub filter](/guides/how-to-clean-hot-tub-filter).
6. **Refill through the filter compartment.** This helps push air out of the plumbing and reduces airlocks. A pre-filter on the hose removes sediment and some metals.
7. **Restore power** once water is above the minimum fill line.
8. **Balance and sanitize.** Adjust total alkalinity, pH and calcium, then add sanitizer and shock. The [hot tub chemicals beginner's guide](/guides/hot-tub-chemicals-beginners-guide) lists the fresh-fill order.

## Do inflatable and hard-shell tubs need different schedules?

**Inflatables usually need more frequent changes because they hold less water and use smaller filters.** The formula accounts for volume, but smaller filters add extra load.

| Factor | Inflatable | Hard-shell |
|---|---|---|
| Typical volume | ~200–300 gallons | ~300–500 gallons |
| Typical filter | Small cartridge, replaced frequently | Larger pleated cartridge, cleaned and reused |
| Common interval with regular use | ~4–8 weeks | ~3–4 months |
| Drain method | Drain valve to hose; some drain fully when collapsed | Drain valve or submersible pump |

Inflatable owners often line up water changes with seasonal breaks. If you're packing up for winter, our [winterizing guide](/blog/inflatable-hot-tub-winter-prep) covers the full shutdown.

## How can you make hot tub water last longer?

**Keep contaminants out, keep the filter clean and shock regularly.** Anything that reduces the load on the water extends its life.

- Shower before soaking, and rinse swimsuits in plain water.
- Rinse the filter weekly and keep a spare in rotation. Browse [filters and filtration](/category/filters-filtration).
- Shock weekly and after heavy use.
- Keep the cover on to reduce evaporation, which concentrates dissolved solids.
- Consider an enzyme product for tubs that see lots of guests. You'll find these in [chemicals and water care](/category/chemicals-water-care).

For the weekly routine that keeps water healthy between changes, see [how to keep your hot tub water clean](/guides/keep-hot-tub-water-clean).
