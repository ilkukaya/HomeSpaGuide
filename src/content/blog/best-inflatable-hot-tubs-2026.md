Inflatable hot tubs have stopped being a gimmick. The 2026 generation has stiffer 3-ply liners, smarter heater controls and — in a few cases — actual freeze-rated operation that didn't exist five years ago. We've spent the last two months living with the top contenders. Here's how they ranked.

## How we tested

We focused on five things home buyers actually care about: out-of-box setup time, heat-up performance, jet feel, off-season durability, and per-day energy cost.

## Coleman SaluSpa Tahiti AirJet — Best for first-time buyers

The Tahiti is the safe default for a reason. The 120-AirJet ring wraps fully around the perimeter (no dead zones), the cover is reinforced where competitors are flimsy, and parts are everywhere if you need them in year three. It is — admittedly — an AirJet-only tub, so don't expect targeted massage.

## Intex PureSpa Plus Greywood — Best polish, best night-time UX

If you'll use your spa after dark and you live in a hard-water area, the Greywood is the right pick. The integrated mineral system cuts chemical use, the control panel is by far the cleanest, and the Fiber-Tech sidewalls feel notably stiffer than the I-Beam designs that dominated five years ago.

## Bestway Lay-Z-Spa Saint Tropez HydroJet — Best for cold-weather use

The dual-jet system and freeze-shield mode make the Saint Tropez one of the few inflatables that's plausibly a winter tub. It's also the largest of the three; if you regularly host four adults, the extra space matters.

## What to skip

The "ultra-budget" sub-$300 imports continue to come and go. We've watched three brands disappear in the last 18 months, leaving owners with no replacement parts. Spend the extra $200 to land on a brand that'll still exist in three years.

## Final pick

For most buyers, the Coleman Tahiti at $500–700 is the right answer. For the night-owl crowd or hard-water households, step up to the Intex Greywood. For genuine winter use, the Bestway Saint Tropez is the only inflatable we'd trust to run November through March.
