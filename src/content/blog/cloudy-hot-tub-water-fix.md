Cloudy water is the most common hot tub complaint, and it's usually fixable in a day. The trick is to diagnose before you dose. Pouring in random products tends to make things worse, because several of the causes share the same symptom but need opposite fixes.

## Why is my hot tub water cloudy?

**Cloudy water usually means low or overwhelmed sanitizer, high pH or alkalinity, a dirty filter, or too many dissolved solids.** Occasionally it's calcium or metals coming out of solution, especially after a fresh fill.

Here are the common causes and how to recognize each one:

| Cause | What you'll often notice | First fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low sanitizer | Strip shows little or no bromine/chlorine; may smell musty or strongly of chlorine | Shock, then restore sanitizer to range |
| High pH or total alkalinity | pH above ~7.8, alkalinity above ~120 ppm; possible scale on surfaces | Lower alkalinity and pH |
| Dirty or worn filter | Chemistry is fine but water stays hazy; filter looks gray or slimy | Clean or replace the filter |
| Heavy organic load | Cloudiness after a busy weekend; lotion or oily film at the waterline | Shock with cover off, clean filter |
| High calcium hardness | White flakes or scale, gritty feel, often with high pH | Lower pH, use a scale sequestrant, partial drain |
| High total dissolved solids (TDS) | Water is old, feels flat, foam doesn't break | Drain and refill |
| Fresh fill with metals (well water) | Cloudy or tinted water right after filling | Metal sequestrant, pre-filter on fill hose |

## How do you fix cloudy hot tub water?

**Test, balance alkalinity and pH, shock, clean the filter and circulate.** Do it in that order and most tubs clear within a day or two.

### Step 1: Test the water

Use a fresh test strip or liquid kit and check sanitizer, pH, total alkalinity and, if you can, calcium hardness. Standard target ranges from CDC and industry guidance:

| Parameter | Target range |
|---|---|
| pH | 7.2–7.8 |
| Total alkalinity | 80–120 ppm |
| Free chlorine | 3–5 ppm (typical residential label range) |
| Bromine | 3–5 ppm (typical residential label range) |
| Calcium hardness | ~150–250 ppm (common manufacturer guidance) |

Strips degrade once opened, so if the results seem odd, check the expiration date.

### Step 2: Balance alkalinity, then pH

Total alkalinity buffers pH, so adjust it first. If alkalinity is high, a pH decreaser (sodium bisulfate) lowers both. If it's low, an alkalinity increaser (sodium bicarbonate) brings it up. Once alkalinity is in range, fine-tune pH. Our [guide to lowering pH in a hot tub](/blog/how-to-lower-ph-in-hot-tub) covers dosing in small steps.

### Step 3: Shock the water

With the cover off and jets running, add shock per the label. Non-chlorine shock (potassium monopersulfate) oxidizes oils and sweat without spiking chlorine. For a badly clouded tub, many owners use a chlorine shock (dichlor) instead, because it both sanitizes and oxidizes. Leave the cover open for the time the label specifies so gases can escape.

### Step 4: Clean or replace the filter

Pull the filter and rinse it thoroughly. If it's greasy or hasn't been deep-cleaned in months, soak it in a filter cleaner. If it's frayed, crushed or old, replace it. See [how to clean a hot tub filter](/guides/how-to-clean-hot-tub-filter) for the full process.

### Step 5: Circulate and wait

Run the jets or filtration for several hours and let the filter do its work. Retest after 12 to 24 hours. If the water is improving, keep going; if it's unchanged, move on to the next section.

## When should you use a clarifier?

**Use clarifier after chemistry is balanced and the filter is clean, when the water is mostly clear but still has a fine haze.** It's a finishing tool, not a first response.

Clarifier works by clumping microscopic particles into larger ones the filter can catch. That means a clean filter is essential; you'll want to rinse it again a day after using clarifier because it will have trapped a lot. Avoid overdosing, which can itself cause cloudiness. You can find clarifiers and enzyme products in [chemicals and water care](/category/chemicals-water-care).

## When should you drain and refill instead?

**Drain and refill if the water stays cloudy after a full balance-shock-filter cycle, if foam won't break down, or if the water is several months old.**

Total dissolved solids build up from sanitizer, chemicals, sweat and evaporation. Past a certain point, nothing you add will make the water behave. A common industry guideline is to drain when TDS rises about 1,500 ppm above your fill water's starting level. For a calendar-based estimate, see [how often to change hot tub water](/blog/how-often-to-change-hot-tub-water).

Before draining, many owners run a line-flush product through the plumbing to remove biofilm, so the fresh water doesn't get contaminated from the pipes on day one.

## Why does my hot tub keep getting cloudy?

**Recurring cloudiness usually means one part of the routine is slipping: sanitizer levels, filter cleaning or weekly shock.** Fix the routine and the cloudiness usually stops.

Checklist for repeat offenders:

- **Test more often.** Busy tubs can burn through sanitizer in a day or two.
- **Shock weekly and after heavy use.** This is the step most often skipped.
- **Rinse the filter weekly** and rotate in a spare so one is always clean and dry.
- **Shower before soaking.** Lotions, deodorant, makeup and hair products all end up in the water.
- **Rinse swimsuits in plain water.** Laundry detergent residue in fabric is a common source of haze and [foam](/blog/hot-tub-foam-causes-and-fixes).
- **Keep calcium in range**, especially if your area has hard water.
- **Replace an old filter.** Cartridges lose their ability to trap fine particles as the pleats wear. Replacement cartridges are in [filters and filtration](/category/filters-filtration).

## Is cloudy water different in an inflatable hot tub?

**The chemistry is the same, but inflatables tend to cloud faster because of smaller filters and lower water volume.** Every bather adds a larger load relative to the water.

Many inflatable models use small paper cartridges that manufacturers recommend rinsing every few days and replacing frequently. Keeping a stack of spares on hand makes a noticeable difference. Owners of [inflatable hot tubs](/category/inflatable-hot-tubs) often find that simply swapping cartridges more often solves persistent haze.
