Lift the cover, press the jet button and a layer of white suds starts building at the waterline. It's an unappealing sight, but foam is one of the more predictable hot tub problems. It almost always has the same few causes, and fixing the cause is more effective than fighting the bubbles.

## What causes foam in a hot tub?

**Foam is caused by surfactants in the water, mostly soaps, laundry detergent, lotions, cosmetics and body oils, which let bubbles from the jets hold their shape.** Low calcium hardness and old water with high total dissolved solids make it worse.

Pure water can't hold a stable bubble. Add even a small amount of soap-like material, then aerate it with powerful jets, and you get foam. In a small, hot, heavily used body of water, those materials accumulate quickly.

| Source | How it gets in | Prevention |
|---|---|---|
| Laundry detergent | Residue in swimsuits and towels | Rinse suits in plain water; avoid washing them with detergent |
| Lotions, sunscreen, makeup | Carried in on skin and hair | Shower without soap before soaking |
| Hair products | Shampoo, conditioner, gel | Tie up or rinse hair; avoid dunking your head |
| Body oils and sweat | Every soak | Regular shock and filter cleaning |
| Food and drinks | Spills | Keep food and drinks out of the tub |
| Cleaning residue | Wiping the shell with household cleaners | Use spa-specific surface cleaners |
| Low calcium hardness | Soft source water | Keep calcium around 150–250 ppm per common manufacturer guidance |
| High TDS | Water that hasn't been changed in months | Drain and refill on schedule |

## How do you get rid of hot tub foam quickly?

**A spa defoamer knocks foam down within minutes.** It's a fast cosmetic fix for when guests are arriving, but it doesn't remove the contaminants that caused the foam.

Defoamers are usually silicone- or alcohol-based and work by breaking surface tension. Add a small amount per the label with the jets running. Overuse can leave a film that clogs the filter, so treat it as a stopgap rather than a routine product.

## How do you get rid of hot tub foam for good?

**Shock the water, clean the filter, balance the chemistry and, if the water is old, drain and refill.** That sequence removes the foam-causing material instead of masking it.

### 1. Test the water

Check sanitizer, pH, total alkalinity and calcium hardness. Low sanitizer lets organic matter build up. pH out of range makes sanitizer less effective. Standard targets from CDC and industry guidance are pH 7.2–7.8 and total alkalinity 80–120 ppm.

### 2. Shock with the cover off

Run the jets and add shock at the label dose. Non-chlorine shock works well for routine use; many owners reach for a chlorine shock when foam is heavy because it both oxidizes and sanitizes. Leave the cover open as long as the label says.

### 3. Clean the filter

The filter is where oils and residues collect. Rinse it thoroughly and, if it hasn't been deep-cleaned recently, soak it in a spa filter cleaner. Our guide to [cleaning a hot tub filter](/guides/how-to-clean-hot-tub-filter) walks through the full process.

### 4. Correct calcium hardness

If calcium is low, add a calcium hardness increaser in small steps. Soft water is one of the most overlooked causes of persistent foam, especially in regions with naturally soft municipal water.

### 5. Consider an enzyme product

Enzyme treatments break down oils and organic films. Owner reports across r/hottubs describe them as useful for tubs that see heavy use or lotion-wearing guests. You'll find them alongside other products in [chemicals and water care](/category/chemicals-water-care).

## Why does foam keep coming back after I shock?

**Foam that returns within a day or two of shocking usually means the water has too many dissolved solids and needs to be changed.** At that point, chemistry can't keep up.

Every chemical you add, plus sweat, evaporation and sanitizer byproducts, raises total dissolved solids. There's no product that removes them; only fresh water does. A common industry guideline is to drain when TDS rises about 1,500 ppm above your starting fill level. If you don't have a TDS meter, the rule of thumb in [how often to change hot tub water](/blog/how-often-to-change-hot-tub-water) gives you a calendar estimate based on tub volume and use.

Before you drain, a line-flush product run through the plumbing helps clear the biofilm that holds on to residues. Otherwise the new water can start foaming sooner than it should.

## How do you prevent hot tub foam?

**Keep soaps and lotions out of the water, shock regularly and change the water on schedule.** Prevention is almost entirely about habits.

- **Rinse swimsuits in plain water after each use** and avoid washing them with detergent. This is one of the most common hidden sources of foam.
- **Take a quick rinse without soap before soaking** to remove lotions, deodorant and cosmetics.
- **Keep hair out of the water** or rinse it first if it has styling products in it.
- **Shock weekly and after heavy use.** See the weekly routine in [how to keep your hot tub water clean](/guides/keep-hot-tub-water-clean).
- **Rinse the filter weekly** and keep a spare cartridge in rotation. Spares are in [filters and filtration](/category/filters-filtration).
- **Keep calcium hardness in range**, especially with soft water.
- **Use spa-safe cleaners** on the shell and cover, never household sprays that can drip in.

## Is foam different from cloudy water?

**They often share causes, but foam is a surface problem and cloudiness is a water-body problem.** It's common to have both at once.

Foam usually points to surfactants and oils. Cloudiness more often points to sanitizer, pH, alkalinity or filter issues. When you have both, the fix overlaps: balance, shock, clean the filter and consider a water change. Our guide to [fixing cloudy hot tub water](/blog/cloudy-hot-tub-water-fix) covers that side of the problem in detail.

## Does the type of hot tub affect foaming?

**Tubs with air blowers or strong bubble systems show foam more readily, and small inflatables accumulate contaminants faster.** The causes are the same across types.

Many [inflatable hot tubs](/category/inflatable-hot-tubs) use air bubble rings rather than water jets, which introduces a lot of air. Combined with lower water volume, that makes them quick to show foam when the water is loaded with residues. Frequent filter swaps and more regular water changes help.
